The `network()` engine renders node–link diagrams through a dedicated GPU-instanced rendering
lane: nodes are instanced points, links are instanced lines, and directed edges get triangle
arrowheads. It is built for large graphs — the same lane scales to millions of elements with
level-of-detail (coming in later steps).

This example builds a **ring of cliques** — fully-connected clusters of 10 nodes bridged in a
ring — and lets d3gl's in-library **force layout** (`layout({ backend: "force" })`, Barnes-Hut)
place the nodes; no coordinates are supplied. The layout is seeded by **multilevel coarsening**
(heavy-edge matching): the graph is collapsed into a hierarchy of ever-smaller graphs, the tiny
coarsest one is laid out first, and positions are projected back down and refined — far faster
convergence and fewer tangles on clustered graphs than a cold start. The **Nodes** slider scales
from 10 to 1,000,000 to stress-test the layout and rendering. Force layout currently runs on the
main thread, so the largest sizes block briefly while solving — moving it to a Web Worker with
progressive convergence is the next step. Rendering uses WebGL instancing — drag to pan, scroll to
zoom, and toggle **Links** between directed (with arrowheads) and undirected.
