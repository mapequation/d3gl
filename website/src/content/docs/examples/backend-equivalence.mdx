d3gl renders the same scene through three backends. These examples render a scene in all
three at once so any compositing divergence is obvious. Zoom/pan any panel to inspect (the
view mirrors across all three); the ↺ button resets the view.

## Overlapping bordered shapes

A flower of opaque, overlapping discs with thick white borders. Because the discs overlap and
are drawn in order, each disc's border is partly covered by later discs' fills — a sensitive
probe of fill/stroke **draw order**. WebGL used to draw all fills then all strokes (every border
on top); it now composites per drawable like Canvas and SVG.

## Stroke joins & caps

A pie chart (closed wedges) with thick semi-transparent black borders, plus a few open rays
crossing it. The wedge corners exercise stroke **joins** (miter spikes vs bevel cuts vs round
arcs), and the rays' ends exercise **caps** — the translucent border lets you see where a ray
ends on top of a wedge border. Toggle the join, cap, and miter limit: all three backends stay
in lockstep (they used to differ — WebGL beveled everything with butt caps, while Canvas/SVG
mitered at different limits). The default is `bevel`, matching the original WebGL look.
