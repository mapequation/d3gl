`network()`: hover, click and select an **open module by its boundary ring** (#476). With `lod({ moduleBoundary })`, a pointer on the ring the cut draws around an expanded module picks that module — the same id, datum and `members()` as when it is collapsed, plus `datum.open === true` — so `on("hover" | "click" | "select")`, `interactive({ selectable, tooltip })` and `selection()` need no new branch. A ring thinner than 6 px is hit over a 6 px band; a glyph drawn over the ring wins, the deepest of overlapping rings wins, and a pointer inside the disc but off the ring picks no module. With `interactive({ hover })` / a selection, the module's highlight is drawn on its ring, its drawn members stay undimmed, and a selected module keeps its highlight as it opens and collapses. With `interactive({ draggable })` the ring is also a drag handle (#479): a drag that starts on it moves the module exactly as a drag of the module collapsed does — the same leaves held, on every layout backend, with its ring, drawn members and links following — while a press inside the disc but off the ring, or with ⌘ / Ctrl held, still pans. On WebGL, Canvas and SVG; a pointer move tests only the rings the frame drew, allocation-free, and a hover change re-emits only the highlight lane. The highlight lane also keeps its GPU buffers while nothing is highlighted (#478): a hover change updates them in place instead of re-creating about nine buffers per change. A layer that grew past 65,536 circles (2.36 MB) for a large selection is freed once nothing is highlighted. The `interactive()` docs now give the ring colours the code draws (red `#dc2626` for selection and hover, yellow `#eab308` for the subtract-marquee preview).
